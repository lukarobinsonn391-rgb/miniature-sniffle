<h2><strong>Seat Comfort and Dimensions</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Premium Economy seats run 38 to 39 inches of pitch and 18 to 19 inches wide, a real step up from Economy's 31-inch pitch and 17 to 18 inches of width.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The seats recline further than Economy, roughly 8 inches, enough for light sleep, but they don't go flat, so anyone hoping to sleep properly on an overnight flight will still feel the difference.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Business Class is a different category entirely. Standard business seats convert into a fully flat bed, and on Lufthansa's newer Allegris aircraft, Extra-Long Bed seats stretch to 2.20 metres, longer than most people are tall.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Even the classic (non-suite) Allegris business seats offer direct aisle access from every seat, something the older 2-2-2 configuration didn't provide for window passengers.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Cabin Design and Privacy</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is where the two cabins diverge the most. Premium Economy is a dedicated cabin, separate from Economy, but it's an open layout with no dividers or doors between seats.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Business Class on Allegris-equipped aircraft comes in several distinct seat types, which is worth knowing before you book since not every Business seat is the same:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Business Suites</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (front row only): fully enclosed with sliding doors, personal wardrobe, and a monitor up to 27 inches</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Classic Business seats</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">: the new Allegris comfort level, but without the sliding door</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Privacy seats</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">: angled closer to the window for extra seclusion</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Extra-space seats</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">: more work surface for productivity-focused travellers</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Extra-long bed seats</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">: bed lengths up to 2.20 metres</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Double seats</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">: designed for two people flying together, with a shared centre partition that can be opened</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lufthansa's official</span><a href="https://www.lufthansa.com/lhg/de/en/business-class"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Allegris Business Class seat information</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> confirms that the cabin includes different seat types, including First Row Suites, Privacy Seats, Extra Space Seats, and Extra Long Beds of up to 2.20 metres.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Only the front-row suites get sliding doors on current Allegris aircraft, so if full privacy matters to you specifically, it's worth checking the seat map before booking rather than assuming every Business seat comes enclosed.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Baggage Allowance</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Premium Economy includes two free checked bags up to 23kg each, twice the standard Economy allowance.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Business Class goes further, with two checked bags up to 32kg each, giving noticeably more room for heavier luggage without triggering excess fees.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Worth flagging: newer Premium Economy Light fares can reduce this baggage allowance and add seat-selection fees, so it's worth checking exactly what's included before comparing prices against Business.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Dining and Amenities</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Premium Economy meals are a genuine upgrade over Economy, served on proper china with larger portions and a curated menu, plus a welcome drink and an amenity kit with slippers, eye shades, and lotion.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Since a February 2025 menu overhaul developed with chef Johann Later, flights over two hours also offer multiple meal choices, including a Pre-Select option that lets you choose from up to nine dishes in advance.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Business Class dining runs further still, with multi-course meals, wider drink selections, and on longer Allegris routes, dining tables in the suite seats for travellers flying together.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The gap in food quality between Premium Economy and Business is real, but it's smaller than the gap in seating comfort.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Lounge Access and Ground Experience</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Business Class includes lounge access at departure airports as standard.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Premium Economy does not include this by default, though passengers can purchase lounge access separately or gain entry through Miles &amp; More elite status.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For flights covered by EU rules, the</span><a href="https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/passenger-rights_en"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">European Commission's air passenger rights information</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> explains protections covering delays, cancellations, denied boarding, and baggage issues.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Both cabins use the same check-in areas as Economy, so the ground experience gap mostly comes down to lounge access rather than separate check-in lanes.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Price Difference: Is the Jump to Business Worth It?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Premium Economy typically runs 2 to 3 times the cost of an Economy fare on the same route, while Business Class runs 3 to 5 times Economy pricing.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That means the jump from Premium Economy to Business is often steeper in absolute terms than the jump from Economy to Premium Economy, so it's worth thinking about what you're actually paying for at each tier.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Which One Should You Book?</strong></h2>
<p><strong>Choose Premium Economy if:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Your flight is under 8 hours and full lie-flat sleep isn't a priority</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">You want noticeably more comfort than Economy without a Business-level price jump</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">You're travelling with extra baggage but don't need lounge access</span></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Choose Business Class if:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">You're on a long-haul overnight flight and want to actually sleep</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Privacy matters, especially if you can book a front-row suite with a sliding door</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lounge access and full meal service are worth the extra cost to you</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">You're checking heavier or bulkier baggage</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As a rough rule, Premium Economy earns its keep on flights over 6 hours where a bit more room makes a real difference, while Business Class is worth the bigger jump specifically on overnight or ultra-long-haul routes where sleep quality changes how you arrive.</span></p>

<h2><strong>FAQs on Lufthansa Business Class vs Premium Economy</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Is Lufthansa Premium Economy worth it over Economy?&nbsp;</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Yes, for flights over about 6 hours. The extra seat pitch, wider seat, better food, and doubled baggage allowance make a real difference on longer routes, even though the seat doesn't go flat.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Does Lufthansa Premium Economy include lounge access?&nbsp;</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No, not by default. Lounge access is included with Business and First Class, though Premium Economy passengers can purchase lounge access separately.</span></p>
<h3><strong>How much bigger is Lufthansa Business Class than Premium Economy?&nbsp;</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Considerably. Business seats convert to a fully flat bed with up to 70+ inches of pitch, compared to Premium Economy's 38-39 inch pitch that reclines but doesn't go flat.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Do all Lufthansa Business Class seats have privacy doors?&nbsp;</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No. On current Allegris aircraft, only the front-row suites include sliding doors. The rest of the Business cabin uses classic, privacy, extra-space, extra-long-bed, and double seat variants without full enclosure.</span></p>
<h3><strong>What's the baggage allowance difference between the two cabins?&nbsp;</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Premium Economy includes two checked bags up to 23kg each. Business Class includes two checked bags up to 32kg each, giving more room for heavier luggage.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Is it a bigger price jump from Economy to Premium Economy, or Premium Economy to Business?&nbsp;</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Usually the second one. Premium Economy runs roughly 2-3x Economy fares, while Business runs roughly 3-5x Economy fares, meaning the absolute price gap between Premium Economy and Business is typically larger.</span></p>
